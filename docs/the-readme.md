# THE README — exploration (2026-09-12, his ruling: preface, not exposition)

His verdict on the shipped README: it gives the universe away. The
world's core loop is DISCOVERY (see → play → win → move on; the docks
board teaches, not a manual) — and the README was a tour guide that
spoiled every stop. His direction: "give an idea of the fiction and
our universe, like a preface to a book, but not an exposition," and
ONE image, bright and colorful, that showcases the universe the way a
game ad does.

## 1. What a preface does (and an exposition doesn't)

A preface sets a voice, promises a world, hands you one image, and
withholds the plot. The reader should close it knowing how the place
FEELS and nothing about what happens there. Applied here:

- No activity list. No feature bullets. No "what you can do".
- No controls table — the preflight card teaches the keys in-game;
  one line ("the ship will teach you the rest") keeps the mystery and
  makes a promise.
- The fiction speaks first; the out-of-fiction voice gets exactly two
  jobs: say what this is (my portfolio, playable) and where (the
  link).

**B — the narrator** (quiet, bookish):

> Somewhere past the charted systems there is a colony that lost a
> ship with all hands and kept going, because that is what the Belt
> does. Her plates are their deck. Her tanks are their water. The
> lane still runs.
>
> You have a ship, a full tank, and a berth at the docks. Nobody out

Both are ~80 words, name nothing mechanical, and spoil no discovery
(the Dry Weeks, the vigil's function, the hunt, the record — all
absent; only their SHADOWS are present).
